# Support Load Analysis — GlanAir

## Overview

This document analyses the support case volume, category distribution, and load patterns observed during the GlanAir pilot. The analysis is based on 30 documented case records and the 15-record FAQ knowledge base built from recurring questions.

---

## Case Volume by Category

| Category | Cases | % of Total | FAQ Deflectable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product Specifications | 9 | 30% | Yes |
| Health & Allergens | 5 | 17% | Yes |
| Logistics & Collection | 5 | 17% | Partially |
| Pricing & Payment | 4 | 13% | Yes |
| Stock Availability | 4 | 13% | No (dynamic state) |
| Post-Sale Follow-Up | 3 | 10% | No (relationship-dependent) |

**Key observation:** 60% of cases fall into categories where a FAQ response could provide a sufficient first answer. Only stock availability (real-time state) and post-sale follow-up (relationship context) genuinely require a human-handled response.

Questions that appeared more than once across the 15-contact pool:

| Question | Frequency | Category | FAQ Record |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does it help with allergies and dust? | 4+ | Health & Allergens | Q15 |
| Is the filter included in the price? | 3+ | Pricing | Q2 |
| Does it come with a warranty? | 3+ | Warranty | Q7 |
| What size room is it suitable for? | 3+ | Product Specs | Q14 |
| Can I collect in the evening? | 3+ | Logistics | Q5 |
| Is it safe to leave on overnight? | 2+ | Product Specs | Q4 |
| Is it noisy when running? | 2+ | Product Specs | Q13 |
| How much electricity does it use? | 2+ | Product Specs | Q11 |

These 8 questions account for an estimated 60–70% of all pre-sale support volume. Each was answered manually, from memory, on every occurrence.

---

## Support Load by Day of Operation

Support load followed a predictable pattern across the 32-day pilot:

- **Days 1–7 (launch week):** High volume, high question diversity. Early buyers asked exploratory, detailed questions. Many first-contact questions not yet in FAQ base.
- **Days 8–20 (mid-period):** Steady volume, narrower question range. Most questions now variations on established patterns. FAQ base coverage would have been effective here.
- **Days 21–32 (late period):** Increasing stock availability enquiries as units sold down. Urgency-driven questions ("Is it still available?", "Can I collect today?"). Post-sale follow-up cases begin.

## SLA Performance Estimate

The informal < 30-minute response target was maintained through the pilot but was never formally measured. Estimated distribution based on recollection:

| Response Time Band | Estimated % |
|---|---|
| < 15 minutes | ~50% |
| 15–30 minutes | ~30% |
| 30–60 minutes | ~15% |
| > 60 minutes | ~5% |

**Implication:** Approximately 20% of cases likely breached the 30-minute SLA target. Without formal tracking, this was invisible. In a scaled operation, a 20% breach rate would be a significant operational signal requiring staffing or routing changes.

---

## Escalation Events

No formal escalations occurred during the pilot. Two situations that would have triggered escalation in a structured system:

1. **Stock-out notification (19 March 2026):** Five buyers in active pipeline received stock-out notifications. No structured protocol existed. Outreach was ad hoc.

2. **Collection no-show:** One buyer confirmed collection time and did not arrive. Follow-up required. No task was created; the follow-up happened from memory the following day.

---

## Support Efficiency Observations

**Cost per case (time):** Each manually handled case required 5–15 minutes of drafting time. At 30+ cases, total support time investment: approximately 3–8 hours across the pilot.

**Efficiency ceiling:** If volume had doubled to 60 cases, the same approach would have required 6–16 hours of support time — still manageable. At 300 cases, it would have required a dedicated support resource or systematic deflection.

**FAQ deflection economics:** If 50% of cases had been deflected to FAQ/agent handling, the human support load would have been approximately 15 cases — roughly 1–2 hours total. This is the scalability argument for knowledge base investment from day one.
